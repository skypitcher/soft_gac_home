In one sentence

SoftGAC is a one-step generative actor whose internal Gaussian bridge layers make the maximum-entropy regularizer an exact, analytical path KL. It needs no entropy estimates and no backpropagation through a long sampler, and it beats many-step diffusion and flow actors at a fraction of the inference cost.

Motivation

Why generative policies?

In many control tasks there is more than one good action. A robot facing an obstacle can go left or right, but not straight. A Gaussian policy has a single peak: to cover both sides, its mean lands in the middle, right into the obstacle. Diffusion and flow policies can model multimodal, highly non-Gaussian action distributions and keep both good options.

The challenge

Expressive generative policies face two problems

1

Cost vs. performance

Many-step diffusion and flow policies are strong, but they call the same network (\(M\) params) \(N\) times for every action, and training backpropagates through all \(N\) calls. One-step generators are fast and cheap, but their returns usually drop.

2

Intractable entropy

For any implicit generator, one step or many, the action is a transformed noise sample. Sampling is easy, but the density, and hence the entropy, cannot be computed:

\[ \pi_\theta(a\mid s)=\int p(z)\,\delta\big(a-f_\theta(z,s)\big)\,dz \;\;\Rightarrow\;\; \mathcal H(\pi_\theta)=\,? \]
  • Approximate the entropy inexact
    GMM fit (DACER) · Monte Carlo (MaxEntDP) · lower bound (DIME)
  • Replace it with a surrogate indirect
    kinetic energy (FLAC) · Wasserstein distance (FlowRL)

Neither guarantees the true MaxEnt policy (endpoint-equivalence).

Can one policy be truly soft, fast, and better?

Method

Open the box: a soft bridge

SoftGAC is also a one-step generator: about the same number of parameters, and one network call. Inside, the parameter budget is split into \(K\) small Gaussian transition layers (\(\approx M/K\) params each), each used once. Each layer is paired with one step of a fixed maximum-entropy reference process that has no parameters.

Each layer is a Gaussian transition

\[ q_{\theta,k}(z_{k+1}\mid z_k,s)=\mathcal N\big(z_k+h\,u_{\theta,k}(s,z_k),\;2h\,\mathrm{diag}\,\sigma^2_{\theta,k}(s,z_k)\big) \]

Sampled, not a deterministic map. \(h=1/K\), and the base is \(z_0\sim q_{\text{ref}}\propto\prod_i\mathrm{sech}^2 z_i\), whose \(\tanh\) image is uniform.

Reference step (maximum entropy, no parameters)

\[ r_k(z_{k+1}\mid z_k)=\mathcal N\big(z_k-2h\tanh z_k,\;2hI\big) \]

Closed-form KL of each layer (Eq. 24)

\[ \mathcal C_k(s,z_k)=\frac12\sum_{i=1}^{d_a}\Big[\sigma^2_{\theta,k,i}+\frac{(\mu_{\theta,k,i}-\mu_{R,k,i})^2}{2h}-1-\log\sigma^2_{\theta,k,i}\Big] \]

with \(\mu_{\theta,k}=z_k+h\,u_{\theta,k}\) and \(\mu_{R,k}=z_k-2h\tanh z_k\).

Control energy = exact path KL (Lemma 1)

\[ \mathcal C_\theta(s,\tau)=\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\mathcal C_k(s,z_k),\qquad \mathbb E_{\tau\sim P_\theta}\big[\mathcal C_\theta\big]=D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_\theta\,\|\,R) \]

Computed in the same forward pass as the action. No density fitting, no entropy bound.

A drop-in soft actor-critic

Endpoint entropy is replaced by the control energy, used directly in both updates:

\[ y=r+\gamma(1-d)\Big[Q^{\min}_{\bar\phi}\big(s',\tanh z'_K\big)-\alpha\,\mathcal C_{\bar\theta}(s',\tau')\Big],\qquad \mathcal L_{\text{actor}}(\theta)=\mathbb E_{s,\,\tau\sim P_\theta}\Big[\alpha\,\mathcal C_\theta(s,\tau)-Q^{\min}_\phi\big(s,\tanh z_K\big)\Big] \]

The temperature tracks a control budget \(\mathcal C_{\text{target}}=\rho_{\text{ctrl}}Kd_a\). At deployment, the actor follows its mean bridge path.

Theory

Principled: MaxEnt RL in path space

Instead of the intractable action entropy, regularize the whole generation path \(\tau=(z_0,\dots,z_K)\) against the reference process \(R\):

\[ \mathcal J_{\text{path}}=\mathbb E_{\tau\sim P_\theta}\big[Q(s,\tanh z_K)\big]-\alpha\,D_{\mathrm{KL}}\big(P_\theta(\cdot\mid s)\,\|\,R\big) \]

Prop. 1 · The path KL contains SAC's regularizer

\[ D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_\theta\|R)=\underbrace{D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\pi_\theta\|u_R)}_{\text{endpoint KL (SAC)}}+\underbrace{\mathbb E_{a\sim\pi_\theta}\big[D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_\theta(\cdot|a)\|R(\cdot|a))\big]}_{\text{how }a\text{ is generated}} \]

Ideal Thm. 1 · Unconstrained base

\[ \frac{dP^\star_Q}{dR}(\tau\mid s)\propto e^{Q(s,\tanh z_K)/\alpha}\;\Longrightarrow\;\pi^\star_Q(a\mid s)\propto u_R(a)\,e^{Q(s,a)/\alpha} \]

Endpoint-equivalent With uniform \(u_R\), \(\pi^\star_Q\) is exactly SAC's Boltzmann policy. The path term only shapes how each action is reached.

Practical Thm. 2 & Cor. 1 · Fixed base

A real actor fixes its base \(p_0\) (here \(q_{\text{ref}}\)), so only the transitions are tilted. The base adds a constant, and the gap is bounded:

\[ D_{\mathrm{KL}}\big(\pi^\star_{Q,p_0}\,\|\,\pi^\star_Q\big)\le\Delta(s)/\alpha \]

Prop. 2 · The actor loss is a reverse-KL projection

\[ \mathcal L_{\text{actor}}(\theta\mid s)=\alpha\,D_{\mathrm{KL}}\big(P_\theta\,\|\,P^\star_Q\big)+\text{const} \]

Each update moves the bridge toward the ideal soft-optimal bridge.
