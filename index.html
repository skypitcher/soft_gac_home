Expressive, soft, and cheap— could a faster actor also be a stronger one?

Soft generative actor-critic (SoftGAC) challenges two common compromises in generative RL: relying on entropy approximations for softness, and sacrificing performance for speed. By designing the actor’s stochastic structure and soft objective together, it makes soft regularization analytical and generates each action with one network evaluation—outperforming strong iterative diffusion and flow policies on challenging tasks under a comparable total actor parameter budget.

The old bargain is too cramped.

Diffusion and flow-matching policies can represent rich, multimodal action distributions. Putting that expressiveness to work in soft RL faces two obstacles:

  • Softness. Actions are easy to sample, but their marginal densities are often hard to evaluate. Soft regularization therefore typically relies on entropy bounds, estimates, or proxies.
  • Computation. Repeated network evaluations make sampling expensive; differentiating through them also raises training cost. One-step and few-step variants reduce sampling cost, but often sacrifice returns.

A soft objective for expressive implicit actors.

Start with noise. A sequence of lightweight stochastic layers—the bridge—transforms it into an action. Together, these Gaussian layers express rich action distributions and expose analytical local costs against a broad reference process. Learning steers the paths toward high-value actions while controlling these costs, without estimating action entropy.

The resulting path-space soft objective guides both actor and critic updates. With unrestricted path laws and a uniform reference action marginal, its optimum recovers SAC’s Boltzmann policy: the extra regularization shapes how actions are generated while preserving the ideal MaxEnt action distribution. A faster actor could have been
stronger all along.

Within a comparable total actor parameter budget, SoftGAC replaces repeated calls to a shared sampler with lightweight, step-specific layers inside one network. Each action requires one network evaluation. The aim is lower training and deployment costs together with higher returns—not a larger model or a weaker policy.
